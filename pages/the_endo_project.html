00 · Problem

The Diagnostic Barrier

Reproductive health conditions disproportionately affect women in low-resource settings, where diagnostic infrastructure is limited, specialist expertise is scarce, and patient data is systematically underrepresented in global AI research.

Endometriosis alone affects 1 in 9 women of reproductive age, yet the average diagnostic delay is 6 to 10 years, driven not only by biological complexity and symptom non-specificity, but fundamentally by the diagnostic gold standard itself: laparoscopy, a minimally invasive surgical procedure requiring specialized training, operating theatre infrastructure, and significant financial resources.

In high-resource settings, laparoscopy is inaccessible to many. In low-resource settings, it is often unavailable entirely. This creates a diagnostic paradox: the procedure most likely to confirm endometriosis is precisely the barrier preventing early diagnosis.

Beyond endometriosis, conditions such as postpartum psychosis remain severely underdiagnosed globally, with disproportionate impact in African clinical settings where psychiatric and specialist infrastructure is limited.

"The consequence extends beyond delayed care, prolonged diagnostic uncertainty drives psychological distress, allows disease progression unchecked, and frequently results in unnecessary procedures for misdiagnosed conditions."

These reproductive health conditions share a common vulnerability: they depend on diagnostic pathways inaccessible to the populations that need them most.

01 · Research Questions

What this project is trying to answer

RQ1

Can AI-supported non-invasive assessment — integrating symptom screening and imaging analysis — achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to laparoscopy in endometriosis, particularly in low-resource settings where surgical procedures are often unavailable or unaffordable?

RQ2

Can a symptom-text screening layer serve as a viable first-stage triage tool in settings where imaging access is delayed or cost-prohibitive, meaningfully reducing time-to-flag for at-risk patients?

RQ3

Do models trained on Western endometriosis imaging data maintain diagnostic reliability when applied to African clinical populations, and how does uncertainty quantification behave under geographic distribution shift?

02 · Proposed Architecture

A three-stage diagnostic pipeline

The system is designed to be deployable in settings where specialist imaging access is delayed or cost-prohibitive. Each stage is independently useful; together they form a full non-invasive diagnostic pathway as an alternative to laparoscopy.

01

Symptom-text screening layer

A lightweight NLP classifier processing structured symptom and clinical history input as a first-stage triage tool, flagging at-risk patients before imaging is required. Particularly relevant in settings where imaging access is delayed or financially prohibitive, early symptom-based triage prioritizes resources toward patients most likely to benefit.

02

Multimodal fusion module

An uncertainty-aware transformer integrating MRI and ultrasound imaging with structured clinical data. Architectural choices are informed by attention-based fusion literature. Uncertainty quantification is a core design requirement (not an afterthought) enabling the model to signal when it is operating outside its training distribution.

03

Geographic generalization evaluation

A systematic evaluation framework testing diagnostic reliability across Western and African patient subpopulations. Draws on distribution shift methodology developed in the AfricaBench collaboration to surface performance gaps that aggregate metrics would otherwise obscure. Validation against African patient data is a core design requirement, not an afterthought.

03 · Research

Three Research Directions

1

Clinical · Medical Imaging · NLP

Non-Invasive Multimodal Diagnosis of Endometriosis

This project develops an uncertainty-aware multimodal diagnostic system that integrates structured symptom and clinical history data with MRI and ultrasound imaging analysis. The central research question is whether AI-supported non-invasive assessment can achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to laparoscopy while remaining accessible in low-resource settings where invasive procedures are often unavailable or financially prohibitive.

A symptom-text screening layer functions as first-stage triage, enabling at-risk patients to be flagged and risk-stratified before reaching imaging resources. The system is designed to reduce unnecessary specialist referrals for low-probability cases, and prioritize diagnostic resources toward patients most likely to benefit. Validation against African patient data is a core design requirement, not an afterthought.

Data gathering stage · March 2026 · Research design complete
2

Computational Biology · RNA-seq · Molecular Subtyping

NeuroRepro-Seq: Lightweight Pipeline for Molecular Disease Subtyping

My undergraduate honours thesis developed a computationally efficient RNA-seq pipeline for molecular subtype discovery in postpartum psychosis, a reproductive neuroimmune condition that remains critically underdiagnosed in low-resource settings. The pipeline incorporates bootstrap ARI stability validation, Mann-Whitney marker identification, and literature-curated gene signatures across neuroinflammatory, HPA/hormonal, and dopaminergic pathways.

Crucially, the pipeline is optimized for small clinical cohorts (n < 25) and low-memory environments (< 400 MB RAM), making meaningful molecular disease discovery achievable without high-performance computing infrastructure. HDLSS-aware, resource-conscious pipeline design is not a compromise, it is a deliberate methodological choice that expands access to molecular diagnostics in the settings that need them most.

Completed & benchmarked · 2025 · Codebase available ongithub
3

Benchmark Infrastructure · Fairness · Tabular ML

AfricaBench: Benchmark Infrastructure for African Health Data

A recurring barrier to deploying AI in African clinical settings is the absence of representative benchmarks for evaluating model performance and fairness. As part of the AfricaBench collaboration — building the first African-focused benchmark for tabular machine learning spanning healthcare, finance, agriculture, and sports domains — this work investigates geographic representation bias in existing ML benchmarks and develops evaluation frameworks that surface fairness and performance gaps between classical models and LLMs on African datasets.

Before deploying any clinical model in a new geographic context, rigorous benchmarking against locally representative data is not optional. AfricaBench contributes to building that foundation systematically. A related investigation into geographic distribution shift in clinical LLMs is currently under peer review.

Active collaboration · 2026–present · In progress
04 . Vision

Beyond Publication

⬡

Deployment

Following clinical validation and regulatory approval, the endometriosis diagnostic system will be implemented through a privacy-protecting web application and mobile platform, giving women direct access to symptom assessment without requiring a hospital visit as a first step.

⬡

Infrastructure

Working with the Federal Ministry of Health of Nigeria to build reproductive health data infrastructure that does not currently exist, creating the foundation for future diagnostic AI development rooted in African clinical realities.

⬡

Education

Advocating for reproductive health education to be embedded in secondary school curricula alongside existing sex education, because the culture of normalizing pain does not end with better diagnostic tools alone.

It ends when the next generation of girls grow up knowing that their symptoms have names, that diagnosis is possible, and that they deserve care. The research is the foundation. The infrastructure, the policy work, and the education are how it reaches the people who need it most.
