00 · Problem

The Diagnostic Barrier

Reproductive health conditions disproportionately affect women in low-resource settings, where diagnostic infrastructure is limited, specialist expertise is scarce, and patient data is systematically underrepresented in global AI research. Endometriosis alone affects 1 in 9 women of reproductive age, yet the average diagnostic delay is 6 to 10 years — driven not only by biological complexity and symptom non-specificity, but fundamentally by the diagnostic gold standard itself: laparoscopy, a minimally invasive surgical procedure requiring specialized training, operating theatre infrastructure, and significant financial resources.

In high-resource settings, laparoscopy is inaccessible to many. In low-resource settings, it is often unavailable entirely. Surgeons may hesitate to perform it due to its invasive nature and associated risks. This creates a diagnostic paradox: the procedure most likely to confirm endometriosis is precisely the barrier preventing diagnosis.

The consequence extends beyond delayed care — prolonged diagnostic uncertainty drives psychological distress, allows disease progression unchecked, and frequently results in unnecessary procedures for misdiagnosed conditions.

Beyond endometriosis, conditions such as postpartum psychosis remain severely underdiagnosed globally, with disproportionate impact in African clinical settings where psychiatric and specialist infrastructure is limited. These reproductive health conditions share a common vulnerability: they depend on diagnostic pathways inaccessible to the populations that need them most.

04 · Research Questions

What this project is trying to answer

RQ1

Can AI-supported non-invasive assessment — integrating symptom screening and imaging analysis — achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to laparoscopy in endometriosis, particularly in low-resource settings where surgical procedures are often unavailable or unaffordable?

RQ2

Can a symptom-text screening layer serve as a viable first-stage triage tool in settings where imaging access is delayed or cost-prohibitive, meaningfully reducing time-to-flag for at-risk patients?

RQ3

Do models trained on Western endometriosis imaging data maintain diagnostic reliability when applied to African clinical populations, and how does uncertainty quantification behave under geographic distribution shift?

03 · Proposed Architecture

A three-stage diagnostic pipeline

The system is designed to be deployable in settings where specialist imaging access is delayed or cost-prohibitive. Each stage is independently useful; together they form a full non-invasive diagnostic pathway as an alternative to laparoscopy.

01

Symptom-text screening layer

A lightweight NLP classifier processing structured symptom and clinical history input as a first-stage triage tool, flagging at-risk patients before imaging is required. Particularly relevant in settings where imaging access is delayed or financially prohibitive — early symptom-based triage prioritizes resources toward patients most likely to benefit.

02

Multimodal fusion module

An uncertainty-aware transformer integrating MRI and ultrasound imaging with structured clinical data. Architectural choices are informed by attention-based fusion literature. Uncertainty quantification is a core design requirement — not an afterthought — enabling the model to signal when it is operating outside its training distribution.

03

Geographic generalization evaluation

A systematic evaluation framework testing diagnostic reliability across Western and African patient subpopulations. Draws on distribution shift methodology developed in the AfricaBench collaboration to surface performance gaps that aggregate metrics would otherwise obscure. Validation against African patient data is a core design requirement, not an afterthought.
